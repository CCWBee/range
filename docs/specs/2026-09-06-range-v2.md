# RANGE v2: flight model, mouse-aim instructor, textured aircraft, grounded scene

Spec for the second pass over RANGE, the self-contained three.js Typhoon demo. Written 2026-09-06,
revised the same day after a four-lens adversarial review (76 findings folded in; the review's
journal is under the session's workflow directory, the accepted changes are all in this text).
The baseline is commit `9daab90` (the demo as the Codex session left it). Read `CLAUDE.md` and this
file in full before touching code. Design decisions here are settled; an implementer who finds one
unworkable writes down why in its report and in `docs/notes/<stream>.md` and takes the nearest
workable option, it does not re-open the design.

The user's brief, in substance: the flight model feels odd; fly towards the mouse like War
Thunder (a circle on screen, the plane pulls to it) with WASD still working; a small instructor so
you do not fall out of the sky; the aircraft must be a model with a skin, not a monolithic grey
shape; shadows and ground clutter so the scene stops looking blocky; keep the prior approach
(every asset modelled in Blender, textures generated, screenshots matched to the five concept
frames at 60 fps, one self-contained HTML file with no external assets); fix whatever is found on
the way and fill the gaps.

## 0. Conventions and hard rules

- World: +Y up. The runway runs along −Z; heading 000 is −Z (`heading = atan2(forward.x, −forward.z)`,
  increasing clockwise seen from above). The coast is at +X (east), the sea beyond it at y = −7
  (sea level; the airfield sits at −0.34 to 0). The range pads are around (−1000, 0, −4200).
- Body: +X right wing, +Y up, −Z nose. Meshes are authored in Blender directly in this frame
  (Blender's own Z-up is ignored; the exporter copies coordinates verbatim, as `model_range.py` does).
- Aerodynamic sign convention used throughout the spec and the tests: `p` roll rate positive right
  wing down, `q` pitch rate positive nose up, `r` yaw rate positive nose right, `α` positive nose
  above the velocity vector, `β` positive wind from the right (nose left of velocity), `φ` bank
  positive right wing down. The mapping to the three.js body angular velocity `ω` is
  `q = ω.x`, `r = −ω.y`, `p = −ω.z`. Tests assert behaviour (heading increases when banked right),
  never raw signs.
- Control surface signs: elevons and rudder positive trailing edge down and trailing edge left
  respectively (standard: positive elevon is a nose-down moment, positive rudder is a nose-left
  moment); canards positive leading edge up; airbrake 0 closed to 1 open; nozzle 0 closed to 1 open.
- Every division by airspeed uses `max(V, 5)`; the aircraft parked at V = 0 must never produce a
  NaN anywhere (physics, control, HUD).
- British English, no em dashes, anywhere: HUD copy, help panel, comments, docs, commit messages.
  Middle dot `·` remains the title separator.
- No emoji as icons, no pill or badge labels, no decorative dots, no rule lines along headings.
  Every HUD element is an instrument with a job.
- Nothing fetched from the network at runtime. `dist/index.html` must pass the existing build
  assertions (no external `src`/`href`, `connect-src 'none'`). The CSP also blocks `fetch` of
  `data:` URLs, so the bundle decodes its binary with `atob`, never with `fetch`.
- One Blender instance (MCP on 127.0.0.1:9876, `tools/blender_call.py <script.py>` runs a script in
  it, `--shot out.png` takes a viewport screenshot). Only one agent drives Blender at a time.
  Cycles bakes run in a background `blender.exe -b` process, never through the MCP socket (a long
  bake blocks Blender's UI thread and the socket times out mid-bake with no error).
- Keep `window.range.stage(name)`, `window.range.metrics()` and the five stage poses working. The
  visual gate depends on them.

```
moor(x, z) = −0.34 + max(0, |x| − 2200)·0.006·(0.6 + 0.4·sin(0.001·z))

far shore: d > 1800          h = −7 + clamp(0.012·(d − 1800), 0, 8)
pavement: h = 0 inside any rectangle; within 60 m outside one, blend to the formula above with smoothstep on the distance to its edge
```

**Noise** (`fbm2(u, v)` in −1..1, identical in JavaScript and Python; `imul` is 32-bit multiply,
`>>> 0` makes unsigned; Python masks with `& 0xffffffff` after every multiply and XOR):

```
hash(ix, iz, seed): n = (imul(ix, 73856093) ^ imul(iz, 19349663) ^ imul(seed, 83492791)) >>> 0
                    n = imul(n ^ (n >>> 13), 1274126177) >>> 0
                    return n / 4294967296
value(u, v, seed):  ix = floor(u), iz = floor(v), fx = u − ix, fz = v − iz, tx = fx²(3 − 2fx), tz = fz²(3 − 2fz)
                    bilinear blend of hash at (ix, iz), (ix+1, iz), (ix, iz+1), (ix+1, iz+1) with tx, tz
fbm2(u, v):         seed 7; n0 = value(u, v, 7), n1 = value(2u, 2v, 8), n2 = value(4u, 4v, 9)
                    return 2·(n0 + 0.5·n1 + 0.25·n2)/1.75 − 1
```

Both P and B print `terrainHeight` at (0, 0), (−1000, −4200), (900, −3000), (3000, −4000),
(5500, −4000) to six decimals in their notes; the B review compares them (they must agree to 1e−6).

**Speed of sound** `a(h) = 340·sqrt(max(0.75, 1 − h/44300))`; Mach uses true airspeed.

## 2. Stream P: flight model (`physics.js`)

### 2.1 Why the old model felt odd

Attitude was driven by commanded rates with a first-order lag, lift acted on velocity with no
moments, so the nose swung without inertia, the ground was a flag with hacks, and rotation at
take-off was a pitch clamp rather than the elevons lifting the nose about the main wheels. Weight
comes from moments and inertia, so v2 is a rigid body with aerodynamic moments and a rate-command
fly-by-wire loop closing on top of it.

### 2.2 State

`Flight` keeps its public field names where they exist (the renderer and tests read them):
`position` (world m), `velocity` (world m/s), `attitude` (quaternion body to world), `throttle`
(0 to 1.12, 1.0 is maximum dry, above it reheat), `spool` (engine 0 to 1.12 following throttle
with lag), `gear` (commanded), `gearPosition` (0 up to 1 down), `brake`, `airbrake`, `grounded`,
`crashed`, `landed`, `alpha`, `load` (load factor n along body up), `bombs`, `rounds`,
`airborneTime`, `elapsed`, `mass`, `impact`.

New fields, all read by the renderer or HUD:

- `omega` Vector3, body angular velocity rad/s (mapping in section 0).
- `beta` rad, `mach`, `ias` m/s (indicated: `tas·sqrt(ρ/ρ0)`), `verticalSpeed` m/s, `reheat` 0 to 1
  (lit fraction, lags the throttle by 0.6 s), `stallSpeed` m/s (the 1 g indicated stall speed at
  the current mass and sea-level density, from CLmax 1.14 with the elevon trim loss ignored),
  `buffet` 0 to 1, `condensation` 0 to 1, `stall` boolean, `crashReason` string, `stopped` boolean.
- `surfaces`: `{canardL, canardR, elevonL, elevonR, rudder, airbrake, nozzle, steering}`. Angles in
  rad with the signs of section 0, `airbrake` and `nozzle` 0 to 1, `steering` rad nose wheel,
  positive right.
- `legs`: array `[nose, left, right]` of `{contact, compression (0 to 1), load (N), spin (rad/s)}`.
- `onGround` boolean: any wheel in contact.
- `basis()` unchanged: `{forward, up, right}` world unit vectors.
- `reset()`, `step(dt, cmd)` where `cmd = {pitch, roll, yaw, throttle, brake, airbrake}`: `pitch`,
  `roll`, `yaw` are the FCS demands in −1..1 (pitch positive pull, roll positive right wing down,
  yaw positive nose right), `throttle` is a rate −1..1 integrated at 0.40 per second (the old
  behaviour, keep it), `brake` and `airbrake` booleans. Also `setThrottle(value)` for staging.
- Exports beyond the class: `terrainHeight`, `fbm2`, `PAVEMENT`, `onPavement`, `qMax(V)` (pull and
  push limits), `pMax(V, bombs, gearPosition)`, `speedOfSound(h)`, `bombStep`.
- `bombStep(bomb, dt)` stays exported with the same contract (returns true on ground impact, where
  ground is `terrainHeight` at the bomb's x, z, or sea level −7 over water); add fin-stabilised drag
  so the bomb noses over: `velocity *= exp(−0.018·dt)`; the renderer orients the mesh from velocity.

### 2.4 Aerodynamics

Dynamic pressure `q̄ = ½ρV²`. Non-dimensional rates `p̂ = p·b/2V`, `q̂ = q·c̄/2V`, `r̂ = r·b/2V`
(V guarded at 5 m/s). Control deflections in rad: elevon `δe` (±0.44), aileron differential `δa`
(±0.35), rudder `δr` (±0.52). All coefficients are per rad.

Lift: `CL = 0.05 + 3.4·α` for |α| ≤ 0.32; beyond, `CL = sign(α)·max(0.6, 1.14 − (|α| − 0.32)·1.8)`.
Add `+0.35·δe`. Peak 1.14 at 18°; the trimmed 1 g stall at 15.2 t is about 67 m/s (130 kt) and the
approach at 82 m/s (160 kt) sits at α ≈ 11°.

Drag: `CD = CD0·(1 + 2·s(M, 0.88, 1.10)) + k·CL²·(1 + 1.5·s(M, 0.88, 1.10))` with `CD0 = 0.021 +
0.025·gearPosition + 0.07·airbrake + 0.004·bombs`, `k = 0.16`, plus post-stall `+0.4·max(0, |α| −
0.28)`.

Side force `CY = −0.85·β + 0.18·δr`.

Moments (body, aero convention):

- `Cl = −0.06·β − 0.32·p̂ + 0.10·r̂ + 0.11·δa`
- `Cm = −0.02 − 0.30·α − 7.5·q̂ − 1.2·δe − 0.35·s(|α|, 0.30, 0.45)·sign(α)`
- `Cn = 0.10·β − 0.28·r̂ − 0.02·p̂ − 0.09·δr + 0.012·δa`

Forces: lift along `right × velocityDirection` (the existing construction), drag along
`−velocityDirection`, side force along `right`, thrust along `forward`, gravity. Moments
`L = q̄·S·b·Cl`, `M = q̄·S·c̄·Cm`, `N = q̄·S·b·Cn`, mapped into ω components by section 0.
Integration: semi-implicit Euler at 120 Hz (the renderer's fixed step), `ω̇ = I⁻¹(M − ω × Iω)`,
attitude advanced by the quaternion of `ω·dt`, normalised. Clamp |ω| ≤ 6 rad/s. Structural failure
(`crashReason 'airframe'`) at n > 12 or n < −5.

Canards are inside `Cm`; their visible deflection is `canard = −0.6·δe`, and on the ground with
weight on wheels they droop to −0.35 rad (the Typhoon parks them leading edge down).

### 2.5 Fly-by-wire (always on, the airframe's own)

Gain-scheduled on dynamic pressure: `qn = clamp(q̄, 3000, 40000) / 15000`.

Pitch: `qMax(V)` returns `{pull: min(8·g0/V, 1.2), push: min(3·g0/V, 0.6)}` (a pull of 8·g0/V is
n ≈ 9 in level flight, the airframe limit). Demand `q_dem = pitch·pull` for positive input,
`pitch·push` for negative. Alpha limiter as hard caps: `q_dem = min(q_dem, 6·(0.34 − α))` and
`q_dem = max(q_dem, 6·(−0.14 − α))`. Controller `δe = −(0.9·(q_dem − q) + 1.2·∫(q_dem − q)dt) / qn`,
integrator clamped ±0.3, `δe` clamped ±0.44 and rate-limited to 3.5 rad/s. Nose up needs
negative `δe`.

Roll: `pMax(V, bombs, gearPosition) = 4.2 rad/s · clamp(V/120, 0.35, 1) · (1 − 0.5·bombs/4) · (1 −
0.6·gearPosition)`; `δa = 0.8·(roll·pMax − p) / qn`, clamped ±0.35, rate 5 rad/s (the 0.8 gain is
what makes full stick reach 3 rad/s clean; a lower gain settles short).

Yaw: `δr = −yaw · 0.52 · clamp(1 − V/250, 0.15, 1) − 1.5·β + 0.15·r`, clamped ±0.52 (the minus sign
on the pilot term is what makes nose-right input yaw the nose right with `Cn = −0.09·δr`; the `r`
term is the yaw damper that keeps the dutch roll from wagging for five cycles). Nose wheel
steering on the ground `steering = yaw · clamp(0.6·(1 − V/40), 0.05, 0.6)` rad.

### 2.6 Wheels and ground

Three contact points in body coordinates: nose `(0, −1.83, −4.35)`, mains `(±1.15, −1.87, 1.2)`
(the mains sit just aft of the centre of mass, as on the real aircraft; further aft the nose wheel
carries 37 % of the weight and the elevons cannot lift it at rotation speed). Ground height: 0
inside a pavement rectangle (section 1.1), else `terrainHeight`. Each wheel is a spring-damper
along world up: `k` 260 kN/m mains, 160 kN/m nose, damping `c = 2·0.7·sqrt(k·m_share)` with
`m_share` the static share of the mass (nose 0.216, each main 0.392), travel 0.35 m (static: nose
0.20 m, mains 0.225 m, level within 0.2°). Beyond full travel a bump stop of 5k applies; it is a
crash only when compression reaches 1 with a closing speed above 3 m/s. Contact force acts at the
wheel point and produces moments, so the aircraft settles on all three, lifts its nose about the
mains when the elevons say so, and can tip if abused. Rolling resistance μ 0.02 pavement, 0.08
grass; brakes on mains μ 0.45 (wet concrete); lateral tyre force `−8·Fz·slipAngle` saturated at
`0.7·Fz`, with `slipAngle = atan2(v_lat, max(|v_long|, 2))`; nose wheel steered by `steering`.
Every ground friction force is clamped to `m_share·|v|/dt` so a parked aircraft cannot
limit-cycle at 120 Hz. `gearPosition` scales the whole gear (retracted gear gives no contact, and a
fuselage touch is a crash).

Crash (`crashed = true`, `crashReason` set, velocity zeroed, aircraft left where it is):
`'impact'` any wheel contact with vertical speed above 5.5 m/s, or bottoming as above;
`'gear'` contact with `gearPosition < 0.9`; `'attitude'` bank beyond 15° or pitch below −6° at
wheel contact, or the nose tip `(0, −0.3, −8)` or a wingtip `(±5.4, −0.1, 3.9)` below ground;
`'terrain'` on grass above 25 m/s; `'water'` position below −7 with `x > coast(z)`;
`'airframe'` as above. `grounded` is all three wheels in contact for 0.2 s; `landed` is set on the
first touchdown after `airborneTime > 10`; `stopped` is `grounded && speed < 0.5`.

Gear moves in 2.6 s and can only be commanded when no wheel is in contact (the renderer already
enforces the key; the model enforces it too).

### 2.7 Derived cues for the renderer

`buffet = s(α, 0.24, 0.34) · clamp(q̄/8000, 0, 1)`; `condensation = s(α, 0.17, 0.30) ·
clamp((V − 80)/120, 0, 1)`; `stall = α > 0.34 && !onGround`; `surfaces.nozzle = clamp((spool −
0.85)/0.27, 0, 1)`; `surfaces.airbrake` follows the airbrake command with τ 0.5 s; wheel `spin =
groundSpeed / 0.31` while in contact, decaying with τ 4 s after lift-off.

### 2.8 Acceptance tests (`tools/test_flight.mjs`)

Run with `node tools/test_flight.mjs`; every assertion prints `PASS <name> <json>`. Keep the seven
existing tests, retuning their expected numbers to the new model, and add:

1. Take-off in reheat with a pull (pitch 0.5) from 74 m/s: rotation between 70 and 82 m/s, main
   wheels leave the ground between 300 and 700 m of run, positive climb, no crash, α ≤ 0.30 for
   the first 3 s after lift-off and no wheel re-contact.
2. 1 g stall speed at 15.2 t between 58 and 70 m/s (decelerate in level flight with the FCS
   holding α at its limit; record V when height starts falling faster than 3 m/s).
3. Roll rate: full roll input at 200 m/s clean (bombs 0, gear up) reaches |p| ≥ 3.0 rad/s within
   1 s; at 75 m/s with gear down and four bombs it stays below 1.6 rad/s.
4. Energy: a held 6 g turn at 250 m/s, 1000 m, throttle 0.8 (dry) for 10 s bleeds at least 15 m/s.
5. Wheels: parked with brakes on and idle thrust the aircraft settles level (pitch within 1°)
   within 3 s and does not creep (moves less than 0.05 m over 30 s); full brakes from 60 m/s stop
   it inside 900 m.
6. Frame-rate stability: 60 Hz versus 120 Hz stepping differ by less than 5 m after 8 s of the
   take-off script.
7. Rudder: at 160 m/s, `yaw = +1` for 2 s increases heading.
8. Instructor tests (section 3.6), using `aimFromAngles`.
9. A gentle geared touchdown at 80 m/s, −2 m/s, on the runway centreline survives; the same at −7
   m/s crashes with `crashReason 'impact'`; the same with gear up crashes with `'gear'`.

## 3. Stream P: mouse-aim instructor (`control.js`)

### 3.1 Contract and reference frame

```js
export class Instructor {
  constructor()            // mode 'assist' (default) or 'manual'
  mode                     // 'assist' | 'manual'
  state                    // {regime, bankDesired, stallGuard, alphaLimited, gLimited, active}
  update(dt, flight, aim, keys) // returns cmd for flight.step
}
export function aimFromAngles(flight, azimuth, elevation) // world unit vector, for tests and staging
```

`aim = {direction: Vector3 | null, active: boolean}`: `direction` is the world unit vector of the
camera ray through the on-screen cursor, `active` is false when the pointer is not locked (hands
off). `keys = {pitch, roll, yaw, throttle, brake, airbrake}` with the same meaning as `cmd`
(section 2.2), from the keyboard. Throttle, brake and airbrake pass straight through from `keys`
in both modes.

The instructor steers the **velocity vector** to the aim, not the nose: the flight-path marker of
section 5.3 goes to the cursor and the nose sits α above it. This is what makes a centred cursor
hold height (a nose-referenced law pushes the nose down to a camera that is aligned with the
velocity, and dives). The reference direction `v̂` is the velocity direction when V ≥ 40 m/s, else
the body forward.

Errors are measured in the **unbanked path frame**: `z = −v̂`, `x = normalize(worldUp × v̂)` (the
horizontal right of the path), `y = z × x`. With the aim `t` expressed in that frame, `ex =
atan2(tx, −tz)` (positive right), `ey = atan2(ty, −tz)` (positive above), `θ` the angle between
`v̂` and `t`. Because the frame does not roll with the aircraft, bank alone never turns a horizon
target into a pitch demand. For the roll decision the aim is also expressed in **body** axes as
`(bx, by, bz)`.

`aimFromAngles(flight, azimuth, elevation)`: start from `v̂`, rotate by `−azimuth` about world up
(so positive azimuth turns the direction to the right, clockwise from above), then by `elevation`
about the frame's `x` axis (positive up). Tests call it every step from the current state (cursor
semantics) unless a test says it holds a fixed world point.

### 3.2 Assist law

Current bank `φ = atan2(−right.y, up.y)`. Available load factor `n_avail = 1.14·q̄·S/(m·g0)`, bank
ceiling `φ_max = max(0.35, 0.9·acos(clamp(1/n_avail, 0, 1)))`.

Small-angle regime: bank to turn. `φ_des = clamp(6·ex, ±φ_max)`; `p_small = 4·(φ_des − φ)`;
`q_small = 1.2·ey·cos φ + min(0.4, (g0/V)·tan φ·sin φ)` (the second term holds the path level in a
banked turn; the `cos φ` makes a target above the path a push when inverted).

Large-angle regime: roll so the target is above the nose, then pull. `Δφ = atan2(bx, by)` (body
axes; this is already the roll needed, do not subtract the current bank); `p_large = 5·Δφ`;
`q_large = 1.0·θ·max(0, cos Δφ)·clamp((0.30 − α)/0.08, 0, 1)` so it never pulls the wrong way
while still rolling and eases off as α approaches the cap.

Blend `w = s(θ, 0.25, 0.45)`, except in the **push cone**: when `ey < 0` and `|ex| < 0.3` the
target is below the path and near the nose, `w = 0` (push, never roll inverted); between `|ex|`
0.25 and 0.4 fade `w` in with smoothstep so nothing jumps, and a `Δφ` at ±π never reaches `p_dem`.

Combine `p_dem = (1−w)·p_small + w·p_large`, `q_dem = (1−w)·q_small + w·q_large`; clamp `p_dem`
to ±3.5 rad/s.

Keyboard on top (assist mode): while `|keys.roll| > 0.05` the keys own the roll axis (`roll =
keys.roll`, the instructor's `p_dem` is suppressed and `φ_des` tracking pauses), so A and D roll
about the aim and can hold the aircraft inverted; `q_dem += 0.6·keys.pitch·pull` **before** the
caps and the guard below, so the guard still wins; `yaw = keys.yaw`.

Instructor caps, tighter than the FCS, applied after the keyboard term: `q_dem = min(q_dem,
12·(0.30 − α))` (hard alpha cap), `q_dem = min(q_dem, 7·g0/V)` (about 8 g), `q_dem = max(q_dem,
−1.5·g0/V)` (n ≥ −0.5). Convert to FCS demands with `qMax` and `pMax` from `physics.js`:
`pitch = q_dem / pull` (or `/ push` when negative), `roll = p_dem / pMax`, clamped −1..1.

Cursor at centre (θ < 0.02): `φ_des = 0`, hold `ey`. Hands off (`aim.active` false or `direction`
null): the aim is the horizontal projection of the velocity (the heading vector when that is
shorter than 1 m/s), which levels the wings and settles the path to level flight.

### 3.3 Stall guard (the "do not fall out of the sky")

`vs = flight.stallSpeed` (indicated). Guard strength `g = 1 − clamp((ias − vs)/(0.10·vs), 0, 1)`
(1 at or below the stall, 0 above 1.10·vs). Positive `q_dem` is scaled by `(1 − g)`. A latch sets
when `g > 0.7` or `α > 0.32` and releases only when `ias > 1.06·vs` **and** `α < 0.26` (the
hysteresis is what stops a pull from cycling push, pull, push at a few hertz). While latched:
`q_dem = min(q_dem, −0.15)` and `φ_des = 0`. Report in `state.stallGuard`. The guard is off with
any wheel in contact.

### 3.4 On the ground

The ground law applies whenever `onGround` (any wheel), both on the roll and on touchdown, at
once; after lift-off it blends into the flight law over 1 s of `airborneTime`. Roll is
`keys.roll` only. Lateral: `yaw = clamp(3·ex, −1, 1) + keys.yaw` when the target is ahead (`tz <
0`), else `keys.yaw` alone (on the landing roll-out the threshold is behind the aircraft and must
not steer it). Pitch stays 0 below `Vr = 1.12·vs`; at or above `Vr`, when the cursor is above
centre (`ey > 0.02`) or `keys.pitch > 0`, `pitch = min(clamp(8·ey, 0, 0.7), 0.12/pull)` so the nose
rises at about 7°/s (rotation is a deliberate, rate-limited action; a gain alone over-rotates into
the alpha latch and porpoises back onto the wheels).

### 3.5 Manual mode

`mode = 'manual'`: `pitch = keys.pitch`, `roll = keys.roll`, `yaw = keys.yaw`, nothing else. The
FCS limits still apply because they belong to the airframe. Toggle key is I (section 5.4).

### 3.6 Instructor tests

With `aimFromAngles` called every step, 120 Hz, starting at 1000 m, 160 m/s true, clean, throttle
0.8 unless stated (the 75 m/s test is true airspeed at 1000 m; `stallSpeed` is indicated):

- Aim 15° right for 8 s: heading increases by 20° to 90°, height within ±120 m, no crash, bank
  never beyond 75°.
- Start at 60° bank, aim centred: bank within 5° of level inside 4 s.
- At 75 m/s with aim 20° up for 10 s: no crash, α stays ≤ 0.36, `state.stallGuard` exceeds 0 at
  some point.
- Hands off (`active` false) for 20 s at 120 m/s: height changes less than 150 m, bank stays under 5°.
- Aim 12° below for 3 s: pitch falls, bank stays under 15°, load factor stays above −1.
- Aim 60° right for 6 s: bank exceeds 60° within 2 s, heading increases by at least 40°.
- Ground: from parked, throttle to reheat, aim centred: no rotation before `Vr`; aim raised 10°
  after `Vr`: airborne within 20 s, α ≤ 0.30 for 3 s after lift-off, no wheel re-contact.
- Landing: from 4 km out on the centreline at 210 m (a 3° slope), 82 m/s, gear down, throttle
  0.3, aim held on a fixed world point on the threshold; below 12 m above the runway the test
  raises the aim to 3° above the horizontal (the flare) and commands throttle −1; touchdown with
  vertical speed under 3.5 m/s, no crash, then brakes stop it within 1500 m of the touchdown point.
- Manual mode: roll right for 0.3 s then centre for 6 s: heading changes by more than 5° in the
  direction of the roll.

## 4. Stream B: Blender assets and textures

### 4.1 Library format (replaces the JSON-only export)

`tools/export_meshes.py` writes `assets/meshes.json` (manifest) and `assets/meshes.bin` (binary).
It reads UVs per loop (`uv_layer.uv[loop_index].vector`, or `uv_layers.active.data[loop].uv`),
never per vertex, and writes indexed geometry welded on (position to 1e−4, normal to 1e−3, uv to
1e−5, colour bytes).

Manifest:

```json
{
  "version": 2,
  "blender": "5.0.1",
  "materials": {"name": {"colour": [r,g,b], "metallic": 0.4, "roughness": 0.5,
                          "map": "jet_basecolor", "normalMap": "jet_normal", "ormMap": "jet_orm"}},
  "assets": {"asset": [{"material": "name", "vertexCount": n, "indexCount": m,
                        "position": byteOffset, "normal": byteOffset, "uv": byteOffset,
                        "color": byteOffsetOrNull, "index": byteOffset,
                        "bounds": [minx,miny,minz,maxx,maxy,maxz]}]},
  "pivots": {"jet_canard_l": {"point": [x,y,z], "axis": [x,y,z], "range": [0, 1.4]}, ...},
  "terrain": {"nx": 256, "nz": 256, "originX": -12000, "originZ": -14000, "spacing": 110,
              "heights": byteOffset}
}
```

Binary: positions, normals as Float32 ×3, uv Float32 ×2, colour (vertex AO, Uint8 ×3, linear, the
block padded to a multiple of 4 bytes) where present, index Uint32, terrain heights Float32
row-major (z outer, x inner). Every block starts 4-byte aligned. Texture names are file stems under
`textures/` (the renderer and `build.py` resolve `.jpg` first, then `.png`).

Pivots: a part with a pivot is exported **pivot-relative** (every vertex has `point` subtracted),
and the renderer does `mesh.position.copy(point); mesh.quaternion.setFromAxisAngle(axis, angle)`.
`axis` is a unit vector in body axes, chosen by B so that a positive angle (right-hand rule) is
the positive sense of section 0: trailing edge down for elevons, leading edge up for canards,
trailing edge left for the rudder, opening for the airbrake and doors, extending for the gear.
`range` is `[stowed, deployed]` in rad for gear and doors.

UV scheme per asset class: jet parts use the shared atlas; runway, taxiway, apron and markings use
world XZ in metres (`uv = (x, z)`); buildings, shelters, tower, vehicles, containers and coast use a
cube projection at 0.25 uv per metre in body space; `quad`, `flame`, `sky`, `blast` and sprites use
0..1.

Normals: run `bmesh.ops.recalc_face_normals` on every mesh before export. For parts flagged convex
in the script (fuselage sections, canopy, tyres, bombs, tanks) assert that no face normal points
at the part's bounding centre; for concave parts (nozzles with interiors, shelters, hangars, intake
mouths, gear bays) assert by ray parity instead (a ray from each face centre along its normal
leaves the part an even number of times).

Parts, each its own asset with a pivot in `pivots`:

Modelling bar: the fuselage is a lofted section series with a chine, a real intake bump beneath

screenshots from the five stage camera positions (`blender_call.py --shot`); the rear
three-quarter view must read as a Typhoon at a glance.

UV unwrap: all jet parts in one multi-object edit session, select all faces,
`uv.smart_project(angle_limit=math.radians(45), island_margin=0.004)`, then
`uv.pack_islands(margin=0.004, rotate=True, scale=True)`, which is what packs across objects at a
uniform texel density (Smart UV Project alone lays every object out from the same corner and the
bake overwrites one part with another). The exporter rasterises each part's UV triangles into a
512² occupancy grid and asserts no texel is claimed by two parts.

Skin material (one node material shared by the paint parts, with variants for canopy, titanium
and rubber): the line pattern is defined in **object coordinates** (Texture Coordinate node,
Object output) so it is continuous across UV seams: frames every 0.9 m in z, stringers every
0.35 m around the fuselage, wing ribs every 0.8 m in x, access panels as a hashed rectangle set
(seeded); pointiness only modulates edge wear. Base colour: RAF air superiority grey (BS 626,
about sRGB 0.60 0.63 0.66), the existing `airframe.png` as micro grain at 8× tiling, low-visibility
roundels on the wings and intake sides, a two-letter code on the fin, walkway stripes on the wing
roots, exhaust soot aft of the nozzles and along the fuselage sides, grime streaks running aft
from the panel lines, darker radome and dielectric panels, dark titanium nozzle cans, black tyres
and grey hubs on the gear. The line and rivet mask feeds a Bump node into every BSDF Normal input
(without it the normal bake is flat).

Bake (`tools/bake_jet.py`, run as `blender.exe -b assets/RANGE.blend -P tools/bake_jet.py`,
Cycles, OptiX, 64 samples, margin 8 px, 2048², only the jet parts selected, each material carrying
an Image Texture node retargeted to the current pass image): passes (1) `DIFFUSE` with
`pass_filter {'COLOR'}` for base colour; (2) `NORMAL`, `normal_space 'TANGENT'`; (3) `AO`; (4)
`ROUGHNESS`; (5) `EMIT` with the metallic value driven through an Emission shader temporarily
linked to each output (Cycles has no metallic bake type). Save the passes as PNG from Blender,
then in Pillow write `textures/jet_basecolor.jpg` (q 90), `textures/jet_normal.jpg` (q 92, the
panel bumps survive it), and `textures/jet_orm.jpg` packed R = AO, G = roughness, B = metallic,
saved with `subsampling=0` (4:4:4; the default 4:2:0 halves the resolution of the roughness and
metallic channels and smears titanium into paint at the nozzle collars).

The materials in the manifest for the jet reference these three maps: `airframe`, `canopy`,
`titanium`, `rubber`. If the bake is still running when the exporter runs, the exporter still
writes the manifest and the renderer's loader falls back to flat colours for a missing map.

## 5. Stream R: renderer (`src/`)

### 5.1 Modules and bundling

`src/main.js` (bootstrap, loop, `window.range`), `src/loader.js` (manifest + binary → geometries,
textures with fallbacks), `src/world.js` (sky, atmosphere, ocean, terrain, lighting, scenery
placement, clutter instancing, lights), `src/aircraft.js` (assembles the parts, animates surfaces,
gear, nozzles, flames, condensation, lights), `src/effects.js` (bombs, gun, explosions, smoke,
spray, tyre smoke, wrecks), `src/camera.js`, `src/input.js` (keys, pointer lock, cursor),
`src/hud.js`, `src/audio.js`, `src/post.js` (bloom, final grade). ES modules with relative imports.

Bundling rules for `tools/build.py` (they are what makes concatenation work): parse each module's
import list (a regex with DOTALL, imports may span lines), topologically sort `physics.js`,
`control.js` and `src/*.js`, fail on a cycle; wrap each module in a **block** (not a function, so
top-level `await` stays legal in the module script): `{ const {a, b} = RANGE['physics']; ...body
with export keywords removed...; RANGE['stem'] = {a, b, ...}; }`; modules export only functions,
classes and `const` objects, never a reassigned `let` (a live binding becomes a stale copy);
`three` is `const THREE = (() => {...})()` as now. The binary library is inlined as base64 in
`window.RANGE_BIN` and decoded with `atob` into one `Uint8Array`, with `Float32Array`,
`Uint32Array` and `Uint8Array` views at the manifest byte offsets; the colour attribute is set with
`normalized = true`. Textures as data URIs keyed by stem. Target bundle under 25 MB; `build.py`
prints the byte count per texture and per binary block and fails above 40 MB.

Loader material interface: when a material has `ormMap`, the loader sets `roughness = metalness =
1` (three.js multiplies the factors into the texels; the manifest numbers serve only the
flat-colour fallback), points `aoMap`, `roughnessMap` and `metalnessMap` at the one ORM texture
(r160 reads the right channels, no `uv1` needed), and sets colour spaces: `map` sRGB, `normalMap`
and `ormMap` none.

### 5.2 Camera (the weight lives here)

Chase camera aligned to the aircraft's path, not to the mouse. Smoothed forward `fs` is a slerp
toward the aircraft's velocity direction (body forward when V < 40 m/s) with τ 0.35 s; desired
offset in that smoothed frame is `−fs·(24 + 4·clamp(V/300, 0, 1)) + worldUp·6.5`. Filter the
offset in the aircraft's frame (critically damped, ω 6 rad/s) and then place it in world space, so
translation never stretches at speed but rotation lags. Camera up is `mix(worldUp, aircraftUp,
0.3)`. Look at `position + fs·40`, plus 15 % of the angle toward the aim direction. FOV 50° to 58°
with speed. Load factor pulls the camera 0.35 m per g down and back; `buffet` adds 12 Hz noise of
0.06 m per unit. Never below 2.7 m above ground. The pre-start pose stays the low three-quarter
view. Key C still toggles the wider view.

### 5.3 Cursor and aim

Pointer lock is requested only from the canvas `mousedown` handler, never from `start()` or
`stage()`. A `mousedown` while not locked only requests the lock and is not a gun event. Mouse
deltas accumulate into a cursor offset from screen centre (sensitivity 1.0 px per px), clamped to a
circle of radius `0.36·innerHeight` (about 20° off the camera axis at the rim, which is the intended
maximum aim: the rim is a firm 7 to 8 g pull at 400 kt, the inner third a gentle correction; the
large-angle regime of 3.2 is reached by the tests and by the arrow keys). Cursor to world:
unproject the NDC point through the live camera each frame; `aim = {direction, active:
pointerLocked}`.

Pause is idempotent and tied to the lock: `pointerlockchange` to unlocked while running calls
`pause(true)`; the sim runs only while `document.pointerLockElement` is the canvas; Esc exits the
lock (the browser does that) and does not toggle anything itself; a click while paused calls
`pause(false)` and requests the lock again, and if the browser refuses (it does for about a second
after an Esc exit) the game stays paused with the click-to-resume overlay showing and the next
click retries. The PAUSE button and P set `pause(true)` and exit the lock.

Markers: the reticle at the cursor is a 26 px circle with a 2 px centre mark; a 10 px cross sits
on the projected nose direction; the flight-path marker (a 14 px circle with 8 px wings) sits on
the projected velocity vector and is what the instructor steers to the reticle. All three are
plain 1 px lines in the HUD's off-white, no fill, no glow.

### 5.5 World

Lighting for an overcast dusk with a low sun behind thin cloud: hemisphere sky 0xb9c6d3, ground
0x2f3630, 1.2; two DirectionalLights 0xffc890 at 0.25 each from direction `(−0.35, 0.21, −1)`
normalised (elevation about 11°; the atmosphere shader's warm band stays on the horizon beneath
it), `PCFShadowMap` with `shadow.radius 3` (PCFSoft ignores the radius in r160). Shadow cameras are
defined by **ground footprint**: near, 2048², a 70 m square around the aircraft; far, 2048², an
800 m square centred 250 m ahead of the camera; both boxes sized to cover their footprint at that
sun angle. The aircraft casts into both (it is 36 texels long in the far map, enough for a blob);
scenery taller than 3 m casts into the far map, nothing else casts. Vertex AO (section 4.3) and a
contact shadow under the aircraft (a radial gradient quad the size of the span, opacity
`0.5·clamp(1 − height/6, 0, 1)`) do the grounding that a low sun cannot. Puddle roughness never
below 0.35 so the sun does not print a disc on the apron (the old ramp frame had a glare blob the
concept does not). Environment map as now.

Scenery placement, all from the library, at the coordinates of section 1.1, in `world.js`. Every
repeated asset is **one InstancedMesh per material part** (instance matrices from the placement
list); singletons are merged by material; `castShadow` only on assets taller than 3 m. Fence line
posts every 12 m along the perimeter roads with a gate; blast fences at both runway ends;
windsock by the threshold; runway edge lights every 60 m (white, both sides), threshold bar (green
facing 36, red facing 18), taxiway edge lights (blue) every 30 m, centreline (green) every 30 m;
PAPI on the left of 36 at 300 m in; approach bars at 150 m spacing out to 900 m off the 36
threshold; containers and hesco around the range pads; rocks and groynes along the coast; a tree
belt of `tree_card` behind the service blocks; the 40 town lights on the far shore.

Lights: every airfield lamp is an emissive quad plus a glow sprite, one InstancedMesh per colour.
The glow uses a small instanced billboard vertex shader (`centre = modelViewMatrix *
instanceMatrix * vec4(0,0,0,1); gl_Position = projectionMatrix * (centre + vec4(position.xy *
size, 0, 0))`, size from `instanceMatrix[0].x`); the existing per-object quaternion trick does not
survive instancing. They are on from the start (it is dusk). The landing light is a SpotLight that
stays in the scene from load with its intensity driven by `gearPosition` (adding or removing a
light recompiles every material).

Clutter: `grass_tuft` and `gorse` crossed-quad sprites, alpha tested, instanced with a hash on

## 7. Performance budget

At 1920 × 1080, pixel ratio 1, on the RTX 5070 Ti: draw calls ≤ 160 (`renderer.info` counts the
main pass, both shadow passes and the post quads), triangles ≤ 2.0 M, two shadow passes, bloom at
one third resolution. Vsync will show 6.94 ms at 144 Hz; the headroom number is `stress(true)`
(pixel ratio 2 with the targets resized, four times the pixels) and must show `slowFraction`
below 0.5 and a mean under 16.6 ms at every stage. Report all three.
